01

Organic reactions & transition-metal catalysis

My research foundation is synthetic organic chemistry, particularly the development and mechanistic study of transition-metal-catalyzed reactions. This work has included nickel-, palladium-, rhodium-, iridium-, ruthenium-, cobalt-, and iron-mediated transformations of strained and unsaturated molecules, often combining experimental reaction development with computation.

I am interested in how catalyst identity, substrate structure, and competing elementary steps control useful synthetic outcomes, and in using mechanistic understanding to connect observed reactivity with new reaction design.

Methods

  • Organic synthesis and methodology development
  • Transition-metal catalysis
  • Reaction optimization
  • Mechanistic experiments
  • Experiment–computation integration
02

Reaction mechanisms & selectivity

Computational studies of organic reaction mechanisms use potential-energy surfaces to connect molecular structure with observed reactivity. My work focuses on locating and validating transition structures, mapping reaction pathways, and decomposing the physical origins of selectivity.

The goal is not simply to reproduce an energetic ordering, but to identify the interactions, distortions, conformational preferences, and electronic effects that determine why one pathway is favored over another.

Methods

  • DFT and electronic-structure theory
  • Transition-state optimization
  • IRC / MEP analysis
  • Activation-strain analysis
  • Noncovalent-interaction analysis
03

Machine-learned molecular models

Machine-learned interatomic potentials provide an emerging route to near-quantum accuracy at substantially reduced computational cost. I investigate their performance for chemically demanding problems in organic chemistry, particularly transition structures, local energetic discrimination, and reaction-pathway modeling.

A central question is where these models can function as reliable DFT surrogates and where conventional electronic-structure calculations remain essential.

Methods

  • Foundation ML interatomic potentials
  • Transition-state geometry optimization
  • Reaction-path calculations
  • Automated computational workflows
  • Model calibration and error analysis
04

Molecular dynamics & nucleic acids

Molecular dynamics provides access to structural ensembles and time-dependent behavior that cannot be represented by a single optimized geometry. I use MD simulations to investigate nucleic-acid structure, conformational heterogeneity, ion-dependent behavior, and the practical requirements for statistically meaningful sampling.

This work connects local structural observables with ensemble-level behavior and asks how simulation design influences the conclusions drawn from biomolecular trajectories.

Methods

  • Classical molecular dynamics
  • RNA / DNA structural analysis
  • Replica-based sampling
  • Convergence analysis
  • Clustering and ensemble statistics
05

Photochemistry & molecular probes

Fluorescent molecular probes translate local chemical environments into measurable optical responses. My work combines TD-DFT, molecular simulation, synthesis, and photophysical experiments to understand how chromophore structure and environment control absorption, emission, charge transfer, and fluorescence enhancement.

These studies connect fundamental photophysics with the design and interpretation of responsive nucleic-acid probes.

Methods

  • TD-DFT / excited-state calculations
  • Absorption and emission analysis
  • Photobasicity and TICT
  • Fluorescent probe design
  • Experiment–theory integration
