About

Chemistry across experiment, computation, and scale.

I work at the interface of experimental organic chemistry and molecular computation, using theory to understand why molecules react, select, reorganize, and emit light.

My research combines quantum chemistry, molecular dynamics, and emerging machine-learned models to study chemical reactivity across scales—from transition states and reaction pathways to conformational ensembles in nucleic acids.

The central objective is practical: develop chemically interpretable computational approaches that extend the scale, speed, and scope of mechanistic questions that can be addressed.

Research

Five connected research directions.

Across projects, the common theme is mechanistic understanding: connecting organic reactivity, molecular structure, dynamics, and predictive models.

Organic reactions & transition-metal catalysis

Experimental and computational studies of synthetic organic reactions, with particular emphasis on transition-metal-catalyzed ring opening, cycloaddition, hydrogenation, hydroacylation, arylation, and cascade chemistry.

Reaction mechanisms & selectivity

Electronic-structure calculations to map catalytic pathways, locate transition states, and rationalize chemo-, regio-, diastereo-, and enantioselectivity.

03

Machine-learned molecular models

Evaluating and deploying machine-learned interatomic potentials as practical surrogates for expensive quantum-chemical workflows in organic chemistry.

MLIPAutomationChemical space

Molecular dynamics & nucleic acids

Simulation and ensemble analysis of RNA and DNA structure, conformational heterogeneity, ionic effects, and sampling convergence.
