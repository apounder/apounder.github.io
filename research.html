Organic synthesis & catalysis

My research foundation is synthetic organic chemistry, with particular emphasis on the development and mechanistic study of transition-metal-catalyzed reactions. This work includes nickel-, palladium-, rhodium-, iridium-, ruthenium-, cobalt-, and iron-mediated transformations of strained and unsaturated molecules, combining reaction development with computational analysis.

I am interested in how catalyst identity, ligand environment, substrate structure, and competing elementary steps control useful synthetic outcomes, and in using mechanistic understanding to guide reaction design.

Reaction mechanisms, selectivity & ML potentials

Computational studies of organic reaction mechanisms use potential-energy surfaces to connect molecular structure with observed reactivity. My work focuses on locating and validating transition structures, mapping reaction pathways, and identifying the physical origins of chemo-, regio-, diastereo-, and enantioselectivity.

Machine-learned interatomic potentials are integrated into this framework as fast surrogates for selected quantum-chemical tasks, including geometry optimization, reaction-path exploration, and local energetic discrimination. A central goal is to establish where these models can accelerate mechanistic workflows while retaining higher-level electronic-structure calculations where they remain necessary.

Molecular dynamics & nucleic acids

Molecular dynamics provides access to structural ensembles and time-dependent behavior that cannot be represented by a single optimized geometry. I use MD simulations to investigate nucleic-acid structure, conformational heterogeneity, ion-dependent behavior, and the practical requirements for statistically meaningful sampling.

This work connects local structural observables with ensemble-level behavior and asks how simulation design influences the conclusions drawn from biomolecular trajectories.

Photochemistry & molecular probes

Fluorescent molecular probes translate local chemical environments into measurable optical responses. My work combines TD-DFT, molecular simulation, synthesis, and photophysical experiments to understand how chromophore structure and environment control absorption, emission, charge transfer, and fluorescence enhancement.

These studies connect fundamental photophysics with the design and interpretation of responsive nucleic-acid probes.
